# Individual blocks below opt specific bots out.

User-agent: *

# Sitemap — Next.js generates this at build/revalidate time from
# `src/app/sitemap.ts`. Advertising it here lets search engines
# discover new content without waiting to crawl the whole site.

# --- Search engines ---

# by Google, Bing, DuckDuckGo, etc. — these lines are redundant with

# --- Answer engines ---
# Explicitly allowed, and deliberately separate from the training
# crawlers below. These fetch a page to cite it or to answer a
# question someone is asking right now; two of them only fetch at all
# because a person asked. Blocking them does not protect the content
# from being trained on — the training crawlers are the ones that do
# that, and they stay blocked. It only removes us from the answer.

User-agent: OAI-SearchBot
Allow: /

User-agent: ChatGPT-User
Allow: /

User-agent: PerplexityBot
Allow: /

User-agent: Perplexity-User
Allow: /

# --- LLM training crawlers ---
# These scrape site content to train large language models. Blocking
# them here doesn't affect search ranking, and — now that the answer
# engines are listed separately above — it no longer costs us a place
# in an assistant's answers either. Those two things were conflated
# when this list was written: OAI-SearchBot, ChatGPT-User,
# PerplexityBot and Perplexity-User sat in this block, which meant
# ChatGPT and Perplexity were not allowed to read a page even when a
# person had explicitly asked them to.
#
# Well-behaved bots respect these rules; malicious scrapers ignore
# them (for those, use rate limiting or bot protection).
#
# `Google-Extended` and `Applebot-Extended` are the specific opt-outs

# Googlebot and Applebot, so blocking them doesn't hurt search
# visibility.
