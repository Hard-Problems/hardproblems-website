# Websites for designers who want to work on hard problems {#websites-for-designers-who-want-to-work-on-hard-problems}

_How do you find work that matters when you barely have time to follow the news? Here are the sites that are worth your time._{.intro}

As an organizational psychologist who thinks systemically, I love to research. In particular, I'm drawn to stories that take a holistic approach to understanding and solving complex problems.

But as the parent of a 4-year old, my time is limited. Ever try discussing employee burnout and structural racism's effect on Black birthing outcomes while dancing to KPop Demon Hunters? I have!

So, what if someone else did the research for you? (And not just your AI.)

Below is our digest of great websites for designers, PMs, and those looking to learn more about where and how to apply their skills for good. Whether you're looking for thought-inspiring content involving impactful careers, seeking a new community, or looking for a job with meaning, these sites can help.

Let us know on [LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/company/hardproblems/) if you know a website that should be included.

PS: For more inspiration, see our list of [books for people who want to work on hard problems](/articles/books-designers-hard-problems).

## Best for <span class="highlight-underline">thoughtful articles</span> {#best-for-thoughtful-articles}

<div class="media-grid">

[![The Stanford Social Innovation Review website](/images/content/website-ssir.jpg)](https://ssir.org/)

<div class="media-body">

### [Stanford Social Innovation Review](https://ssir.org/) {#stanford-social-innovation-review}

This site is a weekly go-to for the Hard Problems team. The SSIR authors craft thought-provoking articles about the charitable sector and social enterprises that challenge conventional wisdom.

</div>

</div>

<div class="media-grid">

[![The Works in Progress website](/images/content/website-works-in-progress.jpg)](https://worksinprogress.co/)

<div class="media-body">

### [Works in Progress](https://worksinprogress.co/) {#works-in-progress}

Works in Progress is "a magazine of new and underrated ideas to improve the world." Authors publish about tech, policy, history, metascience, cities, medical research, aesthetics, transport, energy and more.

</div>

</div>

<div class="media-grid">

[![The Futuress website](/images/content/website-futuress.jpg)](https://futuress.org/)

<div class="media-body">

### [Futuress](https://futuress.org/) {#futuress}

A feminist publisher and learning platform with thoughtful articles on the politics of design. As a queer intersectional feminist platform, Futuress strives to be a home for the people, histories, and perspectives that are often underrepresented, subject to marginalization, and ignored.

</div>

</div>

<div class="media-grid">

[![The Gates Notes website](/images/content/website-gates.jpg)](https://www.gatesnotes.com/)

<div class="media-body">

### [Gates Notes](https://www.gatesnotes.com/) {#gates-notes}

Regardless of your opinion about Bill Gates, his website has thoughtful articles on how some of the most difficult problems in the world might be solved. The site tracks pretty closely to what issues the [Gates Foundation](https://www.gatesfoundation.org/) is focused on now.

</div>

</div>

## Best for seeing <span class="highlight-underline">where you could have impact</span> {#best-for-seeing-where-you-could-have-impact}

<div class="media-grid">

[![The Our World in Data website](/images/content/website-our-world-in-data.jpg)](https://ourworldindata.org/)

<div class="media-body">

### [Our World in Data](https://ourworldindata.org/) {#our-world-in-data}

We think that Our World in Data is one of the best resources for fact-based reporting on the world's biggest issues. They provide excellent insight, with world-class information design, about issues related to the climate emergency, healthcare, poverty, and more.

</div>

</div>

<div class="media-grid">

[![The 80,000 Hours website](/images/content/website-80000-hours.jpg)](https://80000hours.org/)

<div class="media-body">

### [80,000 Hours](https://80000hours.org/) {#80000-hours}

80,000 hours refers to the approximate number of hours a typical worker has in their career — and encourages us to use those hours for maximal good. This site is one of the homes of the "Effective Altruist" movement. It has some excellent guides for thinking about how you can use your skills to make a positive impact in the world.

</div>

</div>

<div class="media-grid">

[![The Probably Good website](/images/content/website-probably-good.jpg)](https://probablygood.org/)

<div class="media-body">

### [Probably Good](https://probablygood.org/) {#probably-good}

Probably Good is a free nonprofit career resource built around one question: how do you pick work that's good for the world and a good fit for you? Its core is an eight-chapter career guide on choosing a direction, testing it, and landing a job. They treat personal fit just as much as picking a big problem.

</div>

</div>

## Best places to <span class="highlight-underline">find a job</span> {#best-places-to-find-a-job}

<div class="media-grid">

[![The Design Gigs for Good website](/images/content/website-dg4g.jpg)](https://www.designgigsforgood.org/)

<div class="media-body">

### [Design Gigs for Good](https://www.designgigsforgood.org/) {#design-gigs-for-good-jobs}

Design Gigs for Good is so good that we've included them on this list twice — first as a community and second as a job board. They list jobs in a Google Group and on their Slack channel.

</div>

</div>

<div class="media-grid">

[![The Escape the City website](/images/content/website-esc.jpg)](https://www.escapethecity.org/)

<div class="media-body">

### [Escape the City](https://www.escapethecity.org/) {#escape-the-city}

A UK-based group that aims to help people leave soul-sucking jobs in finance and at big corporations. They post excellent senior roles at inspiring companies and charities. The roles aren't design-specific, but they have a lot of great roles for designers, marketers, CFOs, and more.

</div>

</div>

<div class="media-grid">

[![The Fast Forward job board](/images/content/website-ffwd.jpg)](https://jobs.ffwd.org/jobs)

<div class="media-body">

### [Fast Forward](https://jobs.ffwd.org/jobs) {#fast-forward}

An excellent job board focused on jobs at charities, mostly in the USA.

</div>

</div>

<div class="media-grid">

[![The 80,000 Hours website](/images/content/website-80000-hours.jpg)](https://jobs.80000hours.org/)

<div class="media-body">

### [80,000 Hours job board](https://jobs.80000hours.org/) {#80000-hours-job-board}

The Effective Altruist site has a large jobs board with many technology and design roles.

</div>

</div>

## Best <span class="highlight-underline">communities</span> {#best-communities}

<div class="media-grid">

[![The OneHealth Tech website](/images/content/website-one-health-tech.jpg)](https://www.onehealthtech.com/)

<div class="media-body">

### [OneHealth Tech](https://www.onehealthtech.com/) {#onehealth-tech}

A wonderful global community of technologists who want to work in healthcare and healthcare professionals who want to learn more about technology. We've attended several in-person events and we're members of their active Slack community.

</div>

</div>

<div class="media-grid">

[![The Design Gigs for Good website](/images/content/website-dg4g.jpg)](https://www.designgigsforgood.org/)

<div class="media-body">

### [Design Gigs for Good](https://www.designgigsforgood.org/) {#design-gigs-for-good-community}

Design Gigs for Good has been around since 2017 and offers both a job board and an excellent free Slack community of designers who want to make the world better. We've talked to their team and are super impressed with what they've accomplished.

</div>

</div>

<div class="media-grid">

[![The UX for Change website](/images/content/website-ux-for-change.jpg)](https://www.ux4change.org/)

<div class="media-body">

### [UX for Change](https://www.ux4change.org/) {#ux-for-change}

UX for Change connects the purpose-driven sector with the UX community. They run in-person meet-ups, events, and hackathons. The team also runs a fellowship program, for designers and future design leaders, to build skills to contribute to social impact projects. UX for Change has groups in the UK, France, US, and Australia.

</div>

</div>

<div class="media-grid">

[![The Climate Designers website](/images/content/website-climate-designers.jpg)](https://www.climatedesigners.org/)

<div class="media-body">

### [Climate Designers](https://www.climatedesigners.org/) {#climate-designers}

We love the name — this is literally a community for designers who want to work on solving the climate crisis. They have a free online community, a job board, and some great resources for shifting your career to focus on the climate.

</div>

</div>

<div class="media-grid">

[![The Elsie website](/images/content/website-elsie.jpg)](https://veryelsie.com/)

<div class="media-body">

### [Elsie](https://veryelsie.com/) {#elsie}

Elsie is a global network for freelancers who want to work on female-centered and sexual health. They have a paid online platform (for a reasonable price) where you can connect with others doing similar work and ask for advice. The team also runs webinars to help your freelance business, weekly check-in sessions, and provide weekly round-ups on current jobs.

</div>

</div>

<div class="media-grid">

[![The Hemingway Report website](/images/content/website-hemingway-report.jpg)](https://www.thehemingwayreport.com/)

<div class="media-body">

### [The Hemingway Report](https://www.thehemingwayreport.com/) {#the-hemingway-report}

An amazing community specifically for mental health operators. The Hemingway Report has an excellent newsletter and an active community.

</div>

</div>
